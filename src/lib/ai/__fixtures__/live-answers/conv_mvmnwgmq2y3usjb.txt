Here's the dress code as written in the manual.

**General expectation**
Employees are to keep a neat, clean, professional appearance at all times, and anyone violating the dress code will be sent home to change into proper work attire and may be subject to disciplinary action, Dress Code for The Company, JBA Policy Manual p.15 [p.15] [p.1]).

**All locations**
- Name tags are worn and visible at all times while working; each employee receives one, All Locations Dress Code, JBA Policy Manual p.15 [p.15] [p.1]).
- Tennis shoes or other full-coverage closed-toe shoes — no sandals, crocs, flip-flops or open-toe shoes; shoes must be tied, Shoes, JBA Policy Manual p.15 [p.15] [p.1]).
- Belts are dark brown or black, and must not hang down or have objects (cell phones, pagers, chains, Belts, JBA Policy Manual p.15 [p.15] [p.1]).
- Hair of all lengths and colors is acceptable as long as it is clean, Hair, JBA Policy Manual p.15 [p.15] [p.1]). Facial hair is acceptable if clean, Facial Hair, JBA Policy Manual p.15 [p.15] [p.1]).
- Reasonable and natural make-up is acceptable and encouraged ("Make-up" Make-up, JBA Policy Manual p.15 [p.15] [p.1]).
- Jewelry that does not interfere with work is acceptable; no dangling earrings or chains, Jewelry/Accessories, JBA Policy Manual p.16 [p.16] [p.1]).
- Large, Tattoos, JBA Policy Manual p.16 [p.16] [p.1]).

**Sun Tan City specifics**
- Any STC employee can wear a branded top, or a black collared or black t-shirt; ASD and above can wear any colored collared shirt; any gray, Shirts, JBA Policy Manual p.17 [p.17] [p.1]).
- Shirts must not be shorter than the beltline or low cut, must be clean and wrinkle-free, with no noticeable patches, Shirts, JBA Policy Manual p.17 [p.17] [p.1]).
- Full-length khaki/black pants or jeans with no holes, rips or patches; no yoga pants, Pants, JBA Policy Manual p.17 [p.17] [p.1]).
- Employees are expected to maintain a consistent tanning schedule in accordance with company appearance standards ("Tanning" Tanning, JBA Policy Manual p.17 [p.17] [p.1]).
